Kingdom Come Youth Conference · Winter 2026

Three directions for the site

Each direction sets the same seven pieces of the real site — nav, hero, About opener, a doctrinal value, the FAQ accordion, the to-be-announced detail rows, and the register band — in a different typeface pairing and palette. The wordmark you see in each nav is that direction's answer for the logo text. Everything is real copy; nothing here is placeholder.

1 · Hymnal 2 · Winter Field 3 · Lantern
Direction 1

Hymnal

The most classical of the three, and the closest in spirit to the crown itself. Garamond has been setting scripture for four hundred years, so it carries the weight without straining for it. Pewter blue appears exactly twice — the register button and the active-nav underline — so black on warm paper stays the identity.

Why Kingdom Come?

The kingdom of God is the good news that, in Jesus Christ, God’s long-awaited reign has arrived. Christ brings God’s story of creation and redemption to its fullness, inviting us out of the false kingdoms of self-rule and into the life God intended from the beginning. In his kingdom, we learn a new way of being human, because our King himself is restoring the world.

Holy Trinity

We believe in the one eternal God—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit—whose gracious reign over all creation is the source and end of all life. From eternity, the triune God has purposed to make all things new and to gather for himself a people who live under his loving rule.

Who can attend?

Middle and high school students from the hosting churches and anyone they bring with them.

BodyKarla, 400–700

Verdict

Pick this if you want the site to feel like it belongs to a church rather than to a conference brand. It ages the best of the three.

Watch out: Garamond’s delicacy means small text needs a size bump, and the whole thing can read formal — possibly older than your audience.

Direction 2

Winter Field

The editorial option. Instrument Serif is a high-contrast display face that gets dramatic at large sizes and disappears at small ones, so it does hero and heading work while Plex Sans handles everything that has to be read. Sage and a cool white keep it in the woods in late December.

Direction 3

Lantern

The warmest and the most youth-facing. Fraunces is a soft serif with a deliberate wobble in its curves — friendly without being childish. This is the only direction where the ink is navy rather than black, and the only one whose register button is a warm fill instead of a dark pill.

Verdict

Pick this if the single most important word is welcoming. The navy also nods to the retreat sweatshirts.

Watch out: navy ink plus a gold accent is close to school-spirit territory — it needs discipline about where the wheat is allowed to appear.

How to answer: you don't have to take one whole. The four separable decisions are the wordmark face, the display face for headings, the body face, and the accent color — mixing across directions is normal. Telling me "Hymnal's wordmark, Winter Field's body, Lantern's accent" is a perfectly good answer.

What I'd pick: Hymnal, with one change — its body face swapped for Winter Field's IBM Plex Sans. EB Garamond next to that crown is the most honest match for a mark built from heraldry and thorns, and the pewter blue stays quiet enough that black-and-white still reads as the identity. Karla is charming but a little quirky under sustained reading, and this site has long doctrinal passages to carry.
